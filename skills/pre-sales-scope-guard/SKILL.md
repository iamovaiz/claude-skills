# pre-sales-scope-guard

Freelancers and small agencies lose more money before the contract than after it. The prospect asks for "a quick proposal", then a market study, then an MVP "so we can see it", and the real work gets done for free while the deal is still a maybe. AI makes this worse: a full research report or a working prototype now takes an afternoon, so it feels cheap to give away. It isn't. It's the thing you sell.

This skill keeps the effort matched to the stage of the deal.

## First, ask what stage the deal is at

| Stage | What is known | Worth producing |
|---|---|---|
| 1. First contact | Who they are, the problem in their words | A short reply and a list of discovery questions |
| 2. Discovery call done | Budget range, decision-maker, the real problem and how success is measured | A proposal: 2 or 3 options with price ranges and what each excludes |
| 3. Paid discovery agreed | A signed small engagement | Research, audits, a prototype, a detailed roadmap |
| 4. Contract signed | Full scope and price | The build |

If the request belongs to a later stage than the deal is at, say so before doing anything: name the stage, name what the request really is, and offer the smaller artifact that fits, plus paid discovery for the rest.

## The facts that make a price defensible

Before any price, timeline or revenue projection, check that you have:

1. **Budget or willingness to pay,** even as a range.
2. **Who decides,** and whether anyone else must approve.
3. **The real problem and how success is measured.** Ask what happens today, step by step, and what number should change.
4. **For commission or revenue-share deals:** the commission rate, their margin, and what they keep after costs.

Missing facts go in a table with three columns: Known (with source), Assumed, Unknown. Any estimate built on an assumed or unknown fact is labelled as such. Never present a figure as solid when the fact behind it is missing.

## Check for misdiagnosis

Before scoping software, ask whether the problem is really a process or data problem: unclear ownership, approvals nobody reads, data entered three times by different people. Software built on a broken process makes it fail faster. If it is mainly a process problem, say so; the first deliverable may be a process fix, not an app.

List hidden scope out loud and mark each item in or out: hardware (point-of-sale terminals, scanners), data migration, integrations with systems nobody has seen, training, support after launch.

## Red flags to name

- Pay by results only, with no data on past results.
- Claims about sales, licences or partners that can't be checked yet.
- Several family members or partners deciding, with no single owner.
- "Just send a proposal" before any discovery conversation.
- Requests that grow after each delivery: proposal, then research, then MVP.

Name a red flag once, plainly. It doesn't end the deal; it changes what you give away.

## AI research is not verification

Running the same question through two or three AI models and treating agreement as proof is a mistake: they draw on the same sources. Mark AI research as unverified until checked against a primary source. Scope any research request explicitly: what it will cover, what it won't, and how long it will take.

## Outputs

- The discovery questions (`templates/discovery-questions.md`), cut to what this prospect hasn't answered.
- The Known / Assumed / Unknown table.
- One recommended next step, with the effort it deserves.
- When the request has outrun the stage: a short paid discovery offer (`templates/paid-discovery-offer.md`), filled in with the user's own price. Never invent a price for them.

## Tone

This is advice to the user, not a refusal. If they decide to do the free work anyway (a strategic client, a portfolio piece), help them do it well, and suggest they put the decision and its limit in writing for themselves.
